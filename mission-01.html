Situation report

Growth happened faster than security planning.

BrightFuture Learning Center supports students, employees, contractors, public visitors, classroom devices, business systems, and cloud services. Its inventory is incomplete, ownership is unclear, and leaders disagree about which assets and data are most critical.

Your team must establish a baseline before any major security improvements begin. You are not being asked to fix every weakness. You are being asked to gather reliable evidence, classify what you find, and recommend the first priorities.

Your question:What must BrightFuture protect first, and what evidence supports that decision?

Mission objectives

  • Identify hardware, software, user, service, and data assets.
  • Assign an owner, location, purpose, and business criticality to each asset.
  • Classify information by sensitivity and potential impact.
  • Recognize technical, managerial, operational, and physical controls.
  • Prioritize risks using likelihood, impact, and business context.
  • Communicate findings in both technical and executive language.
Rules of engagementRequired
  • 01Use only instructor-provided systems, images, records, and scenarios.
  • 02Do not scan or test the school network.
  • 03Record evidence before recommending changes.

Analyst reminder

The most expensive device is not always the most critical asset. Consider confidentiality, integrity, availability, safety, legal impact, and mission impact.

Mission procedure

Complete the operation in six stages.

Each stage produces evidence that supports the final recommendation. Teams should divide responsibilities, but every student must understand the full investigation.

  1. 01

    Brief

    Read the ticket and define the scope

    Identify the organization, approved evidence, required outputs, constraints, and unanswered questions.

    Success check: The team can explain what it is authorized to examine and what it must deliver.
  2. 02

    Discover

    Build the asset inventory

    Record devices, systems, software, accounts, services, data, locations, owners, purposes, and current controls.

    Success check: Every identified asset has enough context to support a security decision.
  3. 03

    Classify

    Determine what matters most

    Rate business criticality and confidentiality, integrity, and availability impact. Explain each high rating.

    Success check: Ratings are based on consequences, not personal opinion.
  4. 04

    Observe

    Audit physical and administrative controls

    Review access, doors, visibility, storage, badges, cameras, procedures, ownership, and evidence handling.

    Success check: Findings distinguish missing controls from controls that exist but are ineffective.
  5. 05

    Prioritize

    Create the initial risk register

    Connect assets, threats, vulnerabilities, likelihood, impact, existing controls, and recommended treatment.

    Success check: The top risks can be defended using evidence and business impact.
  6. 06

    Brief

    Deliver the security baseline

    Submit the technical evidence and a concise executive recommendation naming the first actions leadership should approve.

    Success check: A nontechnical leader can understand the risk and choose an action.
