Course Operations

This class runs like a security team.

Students receive realistic security problems, learn the concepts needed to solve them, complete hands-on operations, and defend their decisions with evidence.

Operating model

Every class follows a repeatable security workflow.

The routine gives students enough structure to work independently while leaving room for investigation, creativity, teamwork, and technical challenge.

01

Mission briefing

Students receive a ticket, alert, request, or incident scenario with goals and rules of engagement.

02

Just-in-time lesson

The class learns only the concepts and techniques needed to begin the current operation.

03

Security operation

Teams configure, test, investigate, document, and collect evidence in an isolated lab.

04

Analyst debrief

Students explain what happened, defend their choices, and connect the work to Security+.

Team structure

Everyone performs the work.

Roles rotate so the same student does not control every keyboard, write every report, or make every decision.

LA

Lead analyst

Organizes the mission, assigns tasks, tracks time, and keeps the team within scope.

TA

Technical analyst

Performs configuration, testing, scanning, packet analysis, and system validation.

EA

Evidence analyst

Collects screenshots, commands, logs, hashes, observations, and validation results.

CA

Communications analyst

Writes tickets, summaries, change records, reports, and the final briefing.

Differentiation

Three levels keep every student moving.

Level 1

Core mission

Guided steps, defined success criteria, required evidence, and a short analysis.

Level 2

Analyst challenge

Less guidance and a new variation that students solve with their team.

Level 3

Senior analyst extension

Open-ended automation, scripting, design, comparison, or peer-challenge work.

Rules of engagement

Practice safely and ethically.

  • 01 Use security tools only inside the authorized course environment.
  • 02 Protect evidence before changing a system or configuration.
  • 03 Use only assigned accounts, targets, networks, and data.
  • 04 Stop and notify the instructor when the scope is unclear.
  • 05 Explain the security impact of every major action.

Assessment model

Grades reward skill, evidence, and reasoning.

Knowledge checks remain part of the course, but they do not outweigh applied security work.
